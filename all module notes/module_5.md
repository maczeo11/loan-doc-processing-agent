## 2. The Real-World Banking Problem (Why Raw PDFs Are a Nightmare)

### The Messy Reality of Loan Dossiers
When applicants submit PDFs to a bank, they do not arrive in clean, standardized formats:
- **The Modern Digital PDF:** Generated by corporate payroll systems (like SAP, Workday, or ADP). These have invisible, clean digital text layers where you can highlight words and press `Ctrl + C`.
- **The Mobile Phone Photo Scan:** The applicant took a crooked, poorly lit photo of a crumpled paper salary slip on their kitchen table, converted the JPEG to a PDF, and uploaded it. There is **zero digital text** inside this file — it is just a grid of colored pixels.
- **The Multi-Page Bank Statement:** 15 pages long with tiny transaction tables, credit/debit columns, and watermarks.

### Why Simple OCR Tools Fail in Enterprise Banking:
1. **Cloud Cost Explosions:**  
   Sending every single page to commercial cloud OCR APIs (like AWS Textract or Google Cloud Document AI) costs $0.015 to $0.05 per page. A 15-page bank statement across 1,000 applicants burns hundreds of dollars in cloud bills within hours.
2. **The "Text-in-the-Air" Problem:**  
   Standard OCR outputs a long, disconnected block of text: *"Net Salary 44200 Balance 34200"*. It loses track of **where on the page** those words were printed. If an auditor asks, *"Where on page 2 did you find 44,200?"*, a naive OCR tool cannot point to the line.
3. **The Coordinate Exploit Vulnerability:**  
   If an OCR parser returns corrupted negative or out-of-bounds coordinates, it can crash browser canvas renderers or cause yellow highlight boxes to render off-screen.

Jeevan's architecture solves all three challenges through **dynamic local routing, strict coordinate bounds checking, and mandatory provenance citations**.

## 3. Key Definitions in Simple Terms (The Module 5 Vocabulary)

| Term | In Simple Words | Real-World Analogy | Role in FinScan AI |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Document Perception** | The ability of software to read and understand text on a page. | Human eyes reading words on a printed document. | Turning PDF files into readable words and numbers. |
| **Native Text Layer** | Invisible digital text embedded directly inside computer-generated PDFs. | Text in a Microsoft Word document saved as PDF. | Extracted instantly in 2ms using PyMuPDF (`fitz`). |
| **OCR (Optical Character Recognition)** | Software that converts an image of letters into actual digital text. | A person looking at a photo of a street sign and typing out the words. | Used for scanned phone photos via PaddleOCR CPU. |
| **OCR Router** | A traffic controller deciding whether a page needs fast native reading or heavy OCR. | A hospital triage nurse deciding if a patient needs a band-aid or an MRI scan. | Decides route page-by-page in `core/extraction/router.py`. |
| **Bounding Box (`BoundingBox`)** | A rectangular frame marking the exact location of words on a page. | Drawing a yellow box with a highlighter pen around a number on paper. | Four coordinates `(x0, y0, x1, y1)` marking the text position. |
| **Normalized Coordinates** | Coordinates scaled between 0.0 and 1.0 (or PDF points) regardless of screen size. | Specifying a location as "center of the page" rather than "300 pixels from left". | Allows React UI to draw highlights accurately on any screen resolution. |
| **`EvidenceRef`** | The digital citation linking a fact to its source document and coordinates. | A legal footnote: *"Page 1, Paragraph 2, Words 4-6"*. | The mandatory proof attached to every single extracted number. |
| **Entity Extractor** | A specialized pattern searcher trained to find specific financial facts. | A detective looking specifically for a serial number on a bill. | Extractors for payslips, bank statements, tax returns, and IDs. |
| **Image Coverage** | The percentage of a PDF page taken up by photos or raw images. | Checking whether a magazine page is mostly printed text or a full-page photo. | If image coverage $> 50\%$, router escalates to OCR. |
| **Mandatory Provenance** | The rule that no fact can exist without proof of where it was read. | A bank auditor refusing to accept an oral claim without seeing the physical slip. | If a fact has no bounding box, it is labeled `UNKNOWN`. |

## 4. Pillar 1: Dynamic OCR Routing (The 3 Perception Routes)

Instead of using a "one-size-fits-all" approach, Jeevan built an **intelligent 3-route waterfall** that evaluates each page individually:

```
                      [Uploaded PDF Page]
                               │
            Is there clean text? (>100 chars, <50% image)
                               │
               ┌───────────────┴───────────────┐
             YES                               NO
               ▼                               ▼
       [ROUTE 1: FAST PATH]           [ROUTE 2: FALLBACK PATH]
       PyMuPDF Native Parser             PaddleOCR on CPU
       • Speed: < 5 ms                   • Speed: 2 to 5 seconds
       • Cost: $0.00 (Zero)              • Cost: $0.00 (Zero cloud fees)
       • Accuracy: 100%                  • Accuracy: ~92–96% on scans
                                               │
                                  Did local OCR fail completely?
                                               │
                                      ┌────────┴────────┐
                                     NO                YES
                                      ▼                 ▼
                                  [COMPLETE]   [ROUTE 3: EMERGENCY ESCALATION]
                                               AWS Textract (Capped at 100 pages,
                                               disabled by default for cost safety)
```

---

### Route 1: The Native Fast Path (PyMuPDF / `fitz`)
* **When it runs:** Whenever a page contains selectable digital text (clearing thresholds of at least 100 characters and less than 50% image coverage).
* **How it works:** It reads the embedded character dictionary directly from the PDF's internal vector stream.
* **Speed:** **2 to 5 milliseconds** per page!
* **Cost:** **₹0.00** (Pure local CPU memory read).
* **Accuracy:** **100% exact** — exactly what the payroll software typed.

### Route 2: The Scanned Fallback Path (PaddleOCR on CPU)
* **When it runs:** Whenever a page fails the native threshold (e.g. a scanned payslip, mobile camera photo, or photocopied ID with < 100 digital characters or heavy image coverage).
* **How it works:** It renders the PDF page into an image in memory, applies image enhancement (contrast adjustment, de-skewing), and runs pre-trained deep learning convolutional networks to detect text boxes and recognize letters.
* **Speed:** 2 to 4 seconds per page.
* **Cost:** **₹0.00** (Runs entirely on the local server CPU — zero external API calls).
* **Accuracy:** 90% to 96% on printed Indian document fonts.

### Route 3: Managed Cloud Escalation (AWS Textract — Emergency Fallback)
* **When it runs:** Only if enabled by an environment variable (`FINSCAN_ENABLE_TEXTRACT=true`) and local OCR fails catastrophically.
* **Strict Safety Ceiling:** Hard-coded budget guard ceiling of **maximum 100 pages for the entire lifetime of the project**, using `DetectDocumentText` only ($0.0015/page). Disabled by default to prevent accidental AWS credit burn!

## 5. Pillar 2: The Golden Architectural Rule (Routing BEFORE Classification)

A common beginner mistake in document processing pipelines is:
> *"Let's classify the document first, and then run OCR on it."*

### 🛑 Why That Fails (The Chicken-and-Egg Paradox):
Think about it:
- How does an ML document classifier know that a file is a Payslip or a Bank Statement?
- It has to read the words! It looks for words like *"Gross Pay"*, *"Account Number"*, or *"Permanent Account Number"*.
- **If a customer uploads a scanned image PDF, there are ZERO digital words in the file!**
- If you run the classifier before OCR, the classifier sees an empty text string: `""`. It will fail completely and classify every scanned document as `UNKNOWN`.

### 🌟 Jeevan's Inviolable Pipeline Order:
```
1. Document Upload ──► 2. OCR Routing & Perception ──► 3. Document Classification ──► 4. Fact Extraction
                       (Converts pixels to words)      (Reads words to label file)    (Extracts numbers)
```
By routing and extracting text first, the ML document classifier always has rich, clean text tokens to make 99% accurate classification predictions!

## 6. Pillar 3: The Coordinate System & Visual Provenance (`EvidenceRef`)

In FinScan AI, extracting a number without its page coordinates is considered a **critical failure**. Every extracted number must be visually verifiable.

### The Coordinate System: How It Works
Think of a standard A4 PDF page as a graph paper measuring **595 points wide by 842 points tall**:

```
(0, 0) [Top-Left Corner] ────────────────────────────────────── (595, 0) [Top-Right Corner]
  │                                                                │
  │                                                                │
  │       "Net Salary: 44,200"                                     │
  │       ╔═════════════════════════════════╗                      │
  │       ║  (x0=120, y0=240)               ║                      │
  │       ║                                 ║                      │
  │       ║               (x1=310, y1=265)  ║                      │
  │       ╚═════════════════════════════════╝                      │
  │                                                                │
  │                                                                │
(0, 842) [Bottom-Left Corner] ──────────────────────────────── (595, 842) [Bottom-Right Corner]
```

### The 4 Coordinates in a `BoundingBox`:
* `x0`: Distance from the left edge to the start of the box.
* `y0`: Distance from the top edge to the top of the box.
* `x1`: Distance from the left edge to the right of the box.
* `y1`: Distance from the top edge to the bottom of the box.

### The Anatomy of an `EvidenceRef`:
Whenever an extractor finds a fact, it bundles it into an `EvidenceRef`:
1. **Document ID:** e.g. `"DOC-PAYSLIP-01"` (Which document was it?)
2. **Page Number:** e.g. `Page 1` (1-indexed, never 0).
3. **Quoted Text Span:** e.g. `"Net Salary: 44,200"`.
4. **Bounding Box:** `(x0=120, y0=240, x1=310, y1=265)`.
5. **Extraction Method:** `"pymupdf_native"` or `"paddleocr_cpu"`.
6. **Confidence Score:** e.g. `0.98` (98% sure).

### 🎨 How Akshaya Uses This in the React UI:
When the bank underwriter opens the FinScan dashboard:
1. The underwriter clicks on the audit card: *"Net Salary: ₹44,200"*.
2. The UI reads the `EvidenceRef` coordinates.
3. The React `pdf.js` canvas instantly scrolls to Page 1 and draws a **glowing yellow rectangular highlight** exactly around the words *"Net Salary: 44,200"*.
4. **Result:** The underwriter verifies the number with their own eyes in **2 seconds** without searching through a 10-page document!

## 7. Pillar 4: The 4 Specialized Domain Fact Extractors

Once text and coordinates are available, Jeevan passes them to **4 specialized domain extractors** in `core/extraction/extractors/`:

---

### 1. `payslip.py` — The Salary Slip Extractor
* 🎯 **What it extracts:**
  - **Employee Full Name:** Uses regex anchors around *"Employee Name:"*, *"Name:"*, *"Emp Name:"*.
  - **Employer / Company Name:** Scans the header banner of the payslip.
  - **Gross Salary:** Searches for terms like *"Gross Pay"*, *"Total Earnings"*, *"Gross Earnings"*.
  - **Net Salary:** Searches for terms like *"Net Salary"*, *"Net Take-Home"*, *"Net Pay"*.
  - **Pay Period:** Identifies the month and year (e.g. *"August 2024"*).
* 🛡️ **Safety Check:** Gross Salary must always be greater than or equal to Net Salary (`Gross >= Net`). If an extraction shows Net > Gross, it flags an anomaly!

---

### 2. `bank_statement.py` — The Bank Statement Extractor
* 🎯 **What it extracts:**
  - **Account Holder Name:** Captures the customer name printed on the account header.
  - **Bank Name & Masked Account Number:** e.g. *"HDFC Bank - XXXXXX1234"*.
  - **Monthly Salary Credit Transactions:** Scans the credit column for corporate payroll descriptions containing keywords like *"SALARY"*, *"NEFT-INFOSYS"*, *"CMS"*, *"PAYROLL"*.
  - **Closing Balance:** Captures the final balance on the statement's ending date.
  - **Bounced Transactions:** Counts instances of *"INSUFFICIENT FUNDS"*, *"ECS BOUNCE"*, or *"CHEQUE RETURN"*.

---

### 3. `tax_return.py` — The Tax Return (ITR-V) Extractor
* 🎯 **What it extracts:**
  - **Assessee Name:** Name of the taxpayer.
  - **PAN Number:** Standard 10-character alphanumeric Indian tax identifier (`[A-Z]{5}[0-9]{4}[A-Z]{1}`).
  - **Assessment Year (AY):** e.g. *"2024-25"*.
  - **Gross Total Income (GTI):** Box 1/Part B total income declared to the Income Tax Department.
  - **Total Tax Paid:** Total taxes deducted (TDS) and paid.

---

### 4. `id_card.py` — The Government Identity Proof Extractor
* 🎯 **What it extracts:**
  - **Applicant Full Legal Name:** Extracted from PAN or Aadhaar card.
  - **Masked Identity Number:** Shows only the last 4 characters for security (e.g. `XXXX-XXXX-1234` for Aadhaar, `XXXXXX1234F` for PAN) to protect customer privacy.
  - **Date of Birth (DOB):** Standardizes dates into `DD/MM/YYYY` format for age verification.

## 9. Architectural Trade-Off & Decision Tables

When your mentor asks *"Why did you design the perception pipeline this way?"*, use these direct comparisons:

### 1. Dynamic Routing (PyMuPDF + Local PaddleOCR) vs. Direct Commercial Cloud OCR (AWS Textract)
| Criteria | FinScan Dynamic Routing (Chosen) | 100% AWS Textract / Cloud OCR |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Processing Speed** | Native digital PDFs parse in **3 milliseconds** | 2 to 6 seconds per page over internet network call |
| **Cloud Cost** | **$0.00** (Runs on local CPU) | $0.015–$0.05 per page ($15 to $50 per 1,000 pages) |
| **Internet Dependency** | Works offline; zero external network reliance | Requires active AWS credentials and stable internet |
| **Budget Safety** | Guaranteed zero unexpected cloud billing surprises | Vulnerable to runaway cloud API bills |
| **Verdict** | **Selected:** Fast, free, and perfectly tuned for multi-page banking documents. | **Rejected:** Prohibitively expensive and slow for native PDFs. |

| **Compliance** | Mandated by project rule: *"Never train custom OCR models"* | Violates AGENTS.md architectural invariant |
| **Verdict** | **Selected:** Pre-trained, open-source industrial engines. | **Rejected:** Strictly banned by team architectural guidelines. |

## 10. Top 15 Mentor & Viva Defense Questions & Answers

### Q1: "What was your specific personal contribution as Jeevan (Member 3)?"
> **Answer:**  
> *"I was the Document Perception and Fact Extraction Architect. I authored the dynamic OCR router in `core/extraction/router.py`, the native PDF layer parser in `native_parser.py`, the local CPU OCR integration in `paddle_parser.py`, and the 4 domain fact extractors in `core/extraction/extractors/` for payslips, bank statements, tax returns, and identity cards."*

### Q2: "Why does OCR routing happen BEFORE document classification?"
> **Answer:**  
> *"Because an ML document classifier cannot classify a file it cannot read. If an applicant uploads a scanned photo or mobile camera picture, there is zero selectable digital text inside the PDF. If we ran classification first, the classifier would receive an empty string and fail. By routing through OCR first, we extract text tokens and coordinates so the classifier has rich text features to make highly accurate predictions."*

### Q3: "What is the difference between Route 1 and Route 2 in your router?"
> **Answer:**  
> *"Route 1 is our fast native path using PyMuPDF. It runs on computer-generated PDFs that already have an embedded digital text layer, extracting text in under 5 milliseconds with 100% accuracy and zero cost.  
> Route 2 is our fallback path using PaddleOCR on CPU. It activates when a page is a scanned photo or has less than 100 characters, using deep learning vision models to read words from the image pixels."*

### Q4: "How does the router decide whether a page needs OCR or native extraction?"
> **Answer:**  
> *"The router inspects two physical metrics on each page:  
> 1. Character count: If digital characters are $< 100$ or word count is $< 10$, it escalates to OCR.  
> 2. Image coverage: If embedded images cover $> 50\%$ of the page area, it indicates a scanned photo, escalating to OCR."*

### Q5: "What is an `EvidenceRef`, and why is it mandatory?"
> **Answer:**  
> *"An `EvidenceRef` is a digital citation model containing the document ID, page number, quoted text span, and bounding-box coordinates `(x0, y0, x1, y1)`. It is mandatory because under the Prime Invariant of FinScan AI, no financial fact is accepted without proof of provenance. If an extractor cannot locate the exact coordinates of a number, it must label it `UNKNOWN`, never a guess."*

### Q6: "How does your coordinate system work?"
> **Answer:**  
> *"We use standard PDF point coordinates where `(0,0)` is the top-left corner of the page and `(page_width, page_height)` is the bottom-right corner. We also normalize coordinates so that Akshaya's React UI can scale them to fit any browser window or mobile screen resolution seamlessly."*

### Q7: "How do you protect against coordinate exploits or browser rendering crashes?"
> **Answer:**  
> *"In `native_parser.py`, we enforce strict coordinate validation bounds: `0 <= x0 < x1 <= page_width` and `0 <= y0 < y1 <= page_height`. If an OCR engine outputs inverted, negative, or out-of-bounds coordinates, our validator clamps or rejects them before they reach the frontend, preventing canvas overflow errors."*

### Q8: "Why didn't you use AWS Textract for all pages?"
> **Answer:**  
> *"AWS Textract costs money ($0.015 to $0.05 per page) and takes several seconds over an internet connection. Most corporate payslips and bank statements are already digital PDFs that PyMuPDF can parse in 3 milliseconds for free on local CPU. We hard-capped Textract under 100 pages and disabled it by default to ensure zero cloud cost overruns."*

### Q9: "How does `payslip.py` extract the net salary accurately?"
> **Answer:**  
> *"It uses anchor-based pattern recognition. It searches for standard payroll keywords like 'Net Salary', 'Net Pay', or 'Take Home Pay', captures the adjacent currency numbers using Indian financial regex patterns (handling commas like `44,200.00`), and verifies that Net Salary is less than or equal to Gross Salary."*

### Q10: "How does `bank_statement.py` identify salary credits among hundreds of transactions?"
> **Answer:**  
> *"It filters transactions in the credit column by matching transaction narration strings against payroll identifiers such as 'SALARY', 'NEFT-SAL', 'CMS/PAYROLL', or known employer corporate names, extracting the credited amount and transaction date."*

### Q11: "What happens if a payslip has a blurred number that OCR cannot read?"
> **Answer:**  
> *"If the confidence score drops below our threshold or the regex fails to find a valid currency amount, the extractor does not hallucinate. It assigns `net_salary = None`. When this reaches Station 4, the rules engine marks the finding as `UNKNOWN`, prompting the human underwriter to manually inspect the document."*

### Q12: "Why didn't you train your own custom OCR deep learning model?"
> **Answer:**  
> *"Training custom OCR models requires tens of thousands of annotated images, weeks of GPU training, and high maintenance overhead. Modern pre-trained engines like PaddleOCR and PyMuPDF are already trained on millions of diverse fonts and documents. Re-using pre-trained models is an explicit architectural rule in AGENTS.md."*

### Q13: "How does your module protect customer privacy (PII)?"
> **Answer:**  
> *"In `id_card.py`, we mask sensitive identifiers before saving. Aadhaar numbers display only the last 4 digits (e.g. `XXXX-XXXX-4821`), and PAN cards display only the last 4 characters in the UI and CAM exports, complying with RBI data privacy guidelines."*

### Q14: "How does Akshaya's React UI connect with your extraction module?"
> **Answer:**  
> *"My extractors output typed `EvidenceRef` models with bounding box coordinates. When Akshaya's frontend loads the PDF via `pdf.js`, it reads those exact coordinates and draws yellow SVG highlight overlays directly on top of the original PDF canvas."*

### Q15: "If the evaluator asks you to summarize Module 5 in one sentence, what will you say?"
> **Answer:**  
> *"Module 5 provides the eyes of FinScan AI — intelligently routing pages between ultra-fast native extraction and local CPU OCR, while capturing financial numbers with exact page coordinates so every single claim can be visually verified by human underwriters."*

### 11.1 Dynamic Document Reclassification & Deletion APIs
* **The New Endpoints (`apps/api/routes/documents.py`):**
  - `PUT /applications/{id}/documents/{doc_id}/reclassify`: Allows underwriters to manually correct a document's classification (e.g. changing an erroneously labeled `unknown` file to a `payslip`).

* **Why it matters:** In real banking, borrowers sometimes upload their documents in the wrong orientation or with misleading filenames. Giving underwriters manual reclassification controls prevents healthy applications from being permanently stuck.

---

### 11.2 Zoom-Aware Bounding Box Auto-Alignment & Scaling
* **The Fix in `BoundingBoxOverlay.tsx` & `PdfViewer.tsx`:** Previously, zooming in or resizing the browser window could cause yellow highlight boxes to drift away from the text.
* **The Upgrade:** Bounding boxes now compute real-time scale factors:
  $$\text{scaleX} = \frac{\text{canvasWidth}}{\text{pageWidth}}, \quad \text{scaleY} = \frac{\text{canvasHeight}}{\text{pageHeight}}$$
  The yellow SVG highlight boxes now remain **pixel-locked** onto the exact words whether the underwriter zooms to 50%, 100%, or 200%!
